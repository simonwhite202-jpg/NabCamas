# Nab Camas: making the most of our house

*Draft proposal for discussion, November 2026.*

## Summary

We propose keeping each family's shares and weeks as they are, while making three things easy: changing next year's weeks before the allocation is signed off in November, selling weeks you won't use, and keeping what they earn. A family that lets all its weeks could pay almost no service charge; one that uses them all pays its full share.

The proposal in brief:

- **Shares and service charges stay as they are.** Each share (about £8,650 of the house at a value of £450,000) carries one week a year and a 52nd of the service charge, £370 in 2026.
- **Next year's weeks can change until November.** A family admin can ask the site admin to change one of their weeks for another family's week. The site admin decides, then signs the allocation off in November, as now.
- **After sign-off, weeks are bought and sold, not swapped.** A family can give a week it won't use to a family that enquired, free, or release it: families who enquired get first refusal, then all members, then trusted friends.
- **Your week, your income.** When a released week lets, the family is credited with what was paid, less a £100 letting cost for the maintenance fund. The credit comes off its service charge, and anything over carries forward.
- **Credits measure fairness (switched off in the portal for now).** Each share is worth 100 credits a year, and weeks cost more in busy seasons, so everyone can see whether each family's weeks match its share.
- **Longer term**, families could buy and sell shares among themselves, so holdings follow how each generation actually uses the house.

A working prototype of an owners' portal already does much of this, so the syndicate can try it before deciding.

## Why change

The house is used about half the time, mostly because holding a week costs nothing and giving it up is awkward. At the moment:

- **There's no shared view of what's free.** Using another family's week means emailing their head of family and waiting for a reply.
- **Weeks are kept just in case.** Plans firm up late, so weeks stay held until it's too late for anyone else to use them.
- **School holidays are a crunch.** Families with school-age children can only come in about 13 weeks of the year, and those weeks are spread unevenly.
- **Some weeks are allocated but never wanted.** Some families have winter weeks they never use. Some larger shareholders use fewer weeks than their share gives them.
- **Needs change over time.** Children grow up, grandchildren arrive, and some families come more or less each year. A fixed pattern of weeks can't keep up.
- **Spare weeks rarely earn anything** to offset costs, because trusted friends can't see what's free.

## Principles

The changes are meant to keep the house a shared family home first, and to make better use of it second.

1. **Families come first.** Members get first call on every week. Trusted friends book only what families don't want.
2. **Fair by share.** What each family uses, pays and receives follows its shares, measured in credits rather than a count of weeks.
3. **Not for profit.** Letting income only offsets what each family pays. £100 from each let goes to the maintenance fund.
4. **Everything is visible.** Every family can see the calendar, who holds what, the credit balances and what each let earned.
5. **Plans can change.** Enquiries, next-year change requests, selling weeks, giving up part of a week, and buying or selling shares let the arrangement change with each generation.
6. **Rewards, not rules.** Families get reminders, and releasing early gives a week longer to find a guest. Nothing is taken from anyone automatically.

## Credits

**For now, credits are switched off in the portal** while the syndicate decides how weeks should be valued (the options are compared in “Nab Camas: four ways to share and trade our weeks”). Every season counts the same, and prices still differ by season. The rest of this section describes how credits would work if adopted.

Each share is worth 100 credits a year, and every week has a credit price that reflects demand. A family spends its credits on the weeks it wants, so taking fewer, busier weeks is as fair as taking more quiet ones.

| Family | Shares | Credits a year |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mackay | 12 | 1,200 |
| Howarth | 11 | 1,100 |
| Ekserdjian | 10 | 1,000 |
| Lunt/Hall | 9 | 900 |
| Bonar | 8 | 800 |
| White | 2 | 200 |
| **Total** | **52** | **5,200** |

Weeks fall into four bands. The syndicate sets the relative values, and the portal scales them so the whole year adds up to 5,200 credits. That also handles years with 53 weeks. At the illustrative values used in this paper, a 2027 week is worth:

- **School holidays:** about 160 credits (13 weeks)
- **Summer:** about 123 credits (7 weeks)
- **Shoulder:** about 94 credits (15 weeks)
- **Winter:** about 42 credits (17 weeks)

With those values, Mackay could take four school-holiday weeks, two summer weeks, two shoulder weeks and a winter week, and stop there. White's 200 credits buy one half-term week or two shoulder weeks.

Credits are a measure of fairness, not money. They can't be bought from the syndicate. When an allocation leaves a family over or under its credits, the site admin can weigh that when deciding change requests, and the syndicate can carry the difference into the next year, so things even out over time.

## The yearly cycle

Each year's allocation starts from the one before, as now. Until the site admin signs it off in November, family admins can ask for changes; after that, weeks are bought and sold rather than swapped.

1. **Roll forward.** The site admin sets up next year's weeks from this year's allocation, with the school-holiday weeks marked. Change requests open as soon as the year is set up.
2. **Changes (until sign-off).** A family that wants to change one of its weeks for another family's week asks the site admin, who decides and makes the change; both families are told.
3. **Sign-off (November).** The site admin signs off the allocation. Any requests still waiting are declined, and the year's service charges are issued alongside.
4. **The year of use.** Weeks are released, enquired about and bought, as described below.

## During the year

Once the year is signed off, weeks change hands only by being given free to a family that asked, or released and bought. Each family marks each of its weeks with one of three settings, so everyone can see what's in use, what might come free, and what's free now.

| Setting | What it means | What other members can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Using it | The family will definitely be there | Nothing; enquiries are closed |
| Undecided | Not sure yet (the default) | Enquire about it; trusted friends can ask to book it |
| Released | The family won't use it | Book it: those who enquired first, then all members, then trusted friends |

**Enquiries: first come, first served**

Any member can enquire about another family's week, unless it's marked *Using it*. This replaces emailing round to ask. The holding family sees who has asked and in what order, and has four options:

- **If the holder gives it to them free:** the family that enquired can use the week. Nothing is paid, there's no letting cost, nothing goes on either family's account, and the other enquiries close.
- **If the holder releases it:** for 48 hours, only those who enquired can take it. If more than one wants it, whoever asked first gets it. After that it opens to whoever the family chose: members and trusted friends straight away, or members first for 7 days.
- **If the holder marks it *Using it*:** enquiries close and enquirers are told, so they can look elsewhere.
- **If the holder isn't sure yet:** they can say so. Enquirers are told and keep their place in the queue.

**Other changes during the year**

- **Questions about any week:** a member can ask the family holding any week a question, even a week they're using. What time are you leaving? Could we arrive a day early? A question isn't a request for the week, so it doesn't join the queue. The family replies on its Enquiries page.
- **Trusted friends can ask too:** a friend can enquire about a week its family hasn't decided about (*Undecided*). The family can let it to them at the friend price, or decline. Friends don't see which family holds a week.
- **Reminders:** family heads are reminded about weeks still *Undecided* 16 weeks and again 8 weeks before arrival, with how many families have enquired. A week is never taken away.
- **Part weeks:** most stays are fortnights, but a family can release part of a week, priced per night. There's a minimum stay of 3 nights, and changeover days are kept, so no unbookable gaps are left. (This comes in a later stage of the portal.)
- **The changeover day is fixed:** Friday in winter and Saturday from about week 12 to week 43, as now.
- **Checking in:** during its week, a family marks itself in residence (Check in) or not in residence on the portal's Overview, so everyone can see whether the house is occupied. Checking in on an *Undecided* week marks it *Using it*.

## Releasing weeks: your week, your income

A family can release any week it won't use. Families who enquired get first refusal, then all members, then trusted friends. When the week lets, the family is credited with what was paid, less a £100 letting cost that goes to the maintenance fund.

**Who can book, and at what price**

- **Members:** can book a released week as soon as it's released (after the enquirers' 48 hours, if anyone enquired), at the same price as trusted friends: there is no member discount.
- **Trusted friends:** they pay the same price, and can book straight away unless the family chose members first. A members-first week opens to them after 7 days, or from 3 weeks before arrival if that's sooner.
- **The family's choice:** a family releasing a week can set its own price, and chooses who can book: members and trusted friends straight away (the default), members first for 7 days, members and its own trusted friends, or members only.
- **Paying:** members pay by bank transfer, or from their family's credit if it covers the price. Credit builds up from weeks a family lets, and a family can top it up by bank transfer at any time.
- **Illustrative prices per week:** school holidays £1,400, summer £1,200, shoulder £800, winter £500. The syndicate sets the real figures.

**How families benefit**

Each let is credited to the family's account when the payment arrives, and the credit comes off its service charge. For example, White holds weeks 7 (half-term) and 8 (winter) in 2027. At the illustrative prices above and the 2026 charge of £370 a share:

| What White does | Credited | Service charge to pay |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keeps both weeks | £0 | £740, in full |
| Releases both; both let to friends (£1,400 + £500) | £1,700 | £0, with £960 carried forward |
| Releases both; only half-term lets, to a member (£1,400) | £1,300 | £0, with £560 carried forward |
| Releases only the winter week, and it lets to a friend (£500) | £400 | £340 |

Credit beyond the service charge stays on the family's account and carries forward, and the family can spend it on weeks other families release. A released week that doesn't let earns nothing, so releasing early, while there's time to find a guest, pays.

**Cancellations.** The guest or the family can cancel a booking that hasn't been paid. Once it's paid, only the site admin can cancel it, and then:

- the let income the family was credited comes back off its account;
- a member who paid from credit gets it back on their family's account;
- a guest who paid by bank transfer is refunded from the syndicate account, under the cancellation policy the syndicate agrees;
- the week goes back on offer, and the guest and the family are told, with the site admin's reason if one is given.

## Money and the not-for-profit position

The syndicate stays not-for-profit: letting income only offsets what each family pays, and every pound is accounted for.

- **Service charges are unchanged.** The 2026 budget (July 2025 to June 2026) is £19,332, charged at £370 per share. Mackay's 12 shares, for example, come to £4,440.
- **Service charge or bank balance.** Each line of a year's budget is either added to the service charge or paid from the syndicate's bank balance. The service charge is the service-charge lines divided by the shares. Lines paid from the bank balance can't add up to more than the latest balance.
- **Letting income reduces the charge.** Each let is credited to the family whose week it was as soon as the payment arrives. Credit beyond the year's charge carries forward.
- **The maintenance fund.** £100 from every let goes to the maintenance fund, towards cleaning, linen and wear and tear. The syndicate sets the figure.
- **One-off costs: upgrades and maintenance.** Anyone in the families can suggest an upgrade, such as a new fridge, a hot tub or a coffee grinder. When a quote comes in for a maintenance issue, it becomes a cost too. These costs wait on the portal's Service charge page, which shows each year's service charge line by line. The site admin can adopt a cost now, paid from the bank balance (within that limit), or put it forward for a year's service charge for the AGM to agree. Otherwise it simply waits. When something more urgent comes up, money set aside for one job can be bumped back to the waiting list or to a later year, or the job cancelled. One-off costs aren't carried into the next year's budget.
- **Guests' extra costs** (cleaning, linen, utilities) can also be built into friend prices, so letting never costs the families money.
- **Everything is on record.** Each booking and top-up has its own payment reference, the site admin confirms each payment received, and every family can see its lets, credit and balance at any time.

It is worth checking with the syndicate's accountant how letting income is treated for tax. Regular letting to friends may need to be declared. Now that income is credited to the family whose week it was, it may count as that family's letting income rather than the syndicate's.

## Looking after the house

Two shared pages help keep the house in good order, for the families and for the trusted friends who stay.

- **Maintenance reports.** Anyone who logs in can report a problem at the house, saying how urgent it is and adding a photo. The site admin is told straight away and keeps each report up to date (In hand, Fixed), with a note to whoever reported it. Families see every report, so nothing is reported twice; trusted friends see their own. When a quote for the repair comes in, a family admin or the site admin adds it to the report, and it waits on the Service charge page to be adopted.
- **The house guide.** How things work at the house: arriving and leaving, heating and hot water, water, Wi-Fi, rubbish, safety and the local area. Anyone in the families can add guidance, and family admins keep it up to date; earlier versions are kept. Anyone who reads it, trusted friends included, can report something wrong or out of date, and family admins are prompted to correct it.

## Adapting across generations: trading usage and shares

Releasing and buying weeks lets families adjust how much they use the house from year to year. Buying and selling shares would let holdings follow longer-term change. Both now work in the portal, but share sales need legal and tax advice before families rely on them.

**Step 1: selling a year's use (no change of ownership)**

- **What it is:** a family that expects to use less releases weeks, and other families buy them at the week's price. This is how weeks change hands during the year.
- **What it changes:** only that year's use. Shares, service charges and votes stay with the seller, who is credited with the price less the letting cost.
- **Records:** the portal records each sale, and the money moves through the family accounts.

**Step 2: buying and selling shares**

- **First refusal:** a family wanting to sell offers its shares on the portal. The other families get first refusal, in proportion to their holdings or in the rotating order.
- **Price:** set by a method agreed in advance, such as an independent valuation of the house each few years divided by 52 shares (about £8,650 a share at around £450,000), or a price agreed at the AGM.
- **Outside buyers:** shares go to someone outside the syndicate, for example a trusted friend's family, only if the other families approve.
- **Splitting a holding:** a family's shares can pass to, or be split between, the next generation's households. Each household then gets its own credits and account; Lunt/Hall already shows how a holding can be shared.
- **Service charges** move with the shares from the date of transfer, and credits are split pro rata for that year.

The budget includes Companies House filing, which suggests the syndicate is a company. If so, share sales are governed by its articles and any shareholders' agreement, and may have stamp duty and capital gains tax consequences. Legal and tax advice is needed before Step 2.

## Transition and timeline

The portal can run 2027 under these rules once the 2027 allocation is signed off. The first full cycle would then be the change requests for 2028, signed off at the end of November 2027.

- **Already working in the portal:**
  - the shared calendar, *where each family sets its own week*s, enquiries (with a free gift or first refusal), and released weeks, with the family choosing who can book and when;
  - trusted friends' enquiries, let income credited to each family, top-ups, paying from credit, and cancellation of bookings by the site admin;
  - sign-off of each year's allocation, and reminders;
  - the budget, service charges, family accounts, and upgrades and maintenance quotes waiting to be adopted;
  - maintenance reports, regular maintenance tasks ticked off by whoever visits, the house guide, and checking in during your week;
  - share offers between families, with first refusal in proportion to holdings, and the share register;
  - this paper as the portal's Manifesto page, open to every family's comments and suggested wording;
  - a questionnaire on NAB's future, with its own questions for the founder, second and next generations, and a summary of the answers by family and generation, and a second questionnaire on how each family would like weeks to be traded;
  - meeting agendas that anyone in the families can add a question to, polls of the family admins (one vote per family) whose results go on the next agenda, and the minutes of past meetings.
- **Still to build:** part weeks.
- **Needed before families rely on it:**
  - emails switched on, which needs a domain and an email-sending account;
  - each family's real logins in place of the demo ones.
